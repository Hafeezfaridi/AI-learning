    Scientific scope

    Models are deliberately semi-quantitative teaching tools: firing rates, kinetics and thresholds are illustrative, scaled to reproduce well-established qualitative relationships (e.g., symptoms emerging at ~50–60% nigral neuron loss and ~70–80% striatal dopamine loss). Not for clinical decision-making or dosing.

    Anatomy pearls

    Striatum = caudate + putamen (input). Lentiform nucleus = putamen + globus pallidus. Output nuclei = GPi + SNr (tonic GABAergic inhibition of thalamus). STN is the only glutamatergic nucleus. SNc is dopaminergic (neuromelanin → dark pigment; pallor at autopsy in PD).

    3 · Dopamine synapse & receptor modulation

    D1 (Gαolf → AC↑ → cAMP/PKA↑) excites direct-pathway MSNs; D2 (Gαi/o → AC↓) inhibits indirect-pathway MSNs. Dopamine therefore pushes both pathways toward more movement.

    4 · Degeneration of SNc dopaminergic neurons

    0%

    Status

    DaTscan-style striatal binding

    Normal = two bright “commas” (caudate head + putamen tail). PD = “periods”: putamen is affected first (posterior > anterior), asymmetrically.

    Selective vulnerability: ventrolateral-tier SNc neurons die first. They are pacemaking, use Cav1.3 L-type Ca²⁺ channels, have huge unmyelinated axonal arbors (~10⁵ synapses each), high mitochondrial load and oxidant-prone dopamine metabolism.

    0

    Histology

    Caveat: Braak's caudo-rostral scheme (and the gut/olfactory “dual-hit” idea) fits many but not all patients. The body-first vs brain-first model (Horsager 2020; Borghammer) proposes that enteric/autonomic-onset disease (early RBD, constipation) differs from brain-first, nigral-onset disease. Lewy pathology burden also correlates imperfectly with neuron loss.

    9 · Neuroinflammation

    Resting microglia (teal, ramified) survey the neuron. Aggregated α-syn, damaged neurons and peripheral inflammation drive an activated, amoeboid state releasing TNF-α, IL-1β, NO and superoxide (e.g., via TLR2/4 → NF-κB → NLRP3 inflammasome).

    10 · Connecting pathology to symptoms

    0%

    TRAP mnemonic

    Tremor (resting, 4–6 Hz) · Rigidity (lead-pipe/cogwheel) · Akinesia/bradykinesia (required for diagnosis) · Postural instability (late). Diagnosis (MDS): bradykinesia + rigidity or rest tremor, supportive criteria (levodopa response, olfactory loss), no exclusions.

    Levodopa pharmacokinetics → motor response

    Blue dashed = relative plasma levodopa. Amber = net dopaminergic motor effect. Green band = therapeutic window (ON); below = OFF; above = dyskinesia. Early disease: striatal buffering smooths the response. Advanced disease: response follows plasma levels, and the window narrows.
