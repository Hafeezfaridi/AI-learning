Descending Pain Modulation

Hafeez Faridi Ph.D.

An interactive illustration of descending analgesia: cortical and brainstem control centers activate spinal inhibitory interneurons. Endogenous opioids, including endorphins, suppress nociceptive neurotransmission in the dorsal horn and reduce ascent of pain signals.

Ready to demonstrate descending inhibition of nociceptive signaling.

1. Descending control

Brainstem circuits, influenced by higher brain regions, send inhibitory projections down to the spinal dorsal horn. This system can reduce the gain of nociceptive processing.

2. Endogenous opioids

Spinal inhibitory interneurons can release endogenous opioids such as enkephalins and endorphin-related peptides. Opioid receptors reduce presynaptic transmitter release and postsynaptic excitability.

3. Suppressed ascent

With reduced glutamate and substance P signaling at the dorsal horn, projection-neuron activation declines and less nociceptive information ascends toward the brain.

Educational simplification: descending pain control includes multiple interacting systems, including periaqueductal gray (PAG), rostroventromedial medulla (RVM), noradrenergic and serotonergic pathways, local inhibitory interneurons, and endogenous opioid signaling. It modulates pain but does not universally eliminate nociception.
