Mechanism of Pain

Hafeez Faridi Ph.D.

A simplified interactive illustration of ascending nociception: tissue injury activates a nociceptor, an action potential travels through first-, second-, and third-order neurons, and neurotransmitters relay the signal at synapses until pain reaches the cerebral cortex.

Ready to animate the ascending nociceptive pathway.

1. Pain sensing

Noxious heat, pressure, or chemicals activate peripheral nociceptors. The first-order sensory neuron carries the encoded signal through the dorsal root ganglion toward the spinal dorsal horn.

2. Spinal transmission

At the dorsal horn synapse, primary afferents release glutamate and may release substance P. The second-order neuron crosses and ascends contralaterally in the anterolateral/spinothalamic pathway.

3. Brain perception

Second-order neurons relay in the thalamus. Third-order thalamocortical neurons project to somatosensory cortex, where pain location and intensity are consciously processed.

Educational simplification: real nociceptive processing includes parallel ascending pathways, interneurons, descending modulation, receptor diversity, and context-dependent neurotransmission.
